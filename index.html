// About Voltage Dividers

A voltage divider uses two resistors in series to produce a fraction of the input voltage. Common uses: level shifting signals, reading potentiometers, biasing transistors. Note: output impedance matters — add a buffer op-amp for precision applications.

// Typical LED Values

Red/Yellow/Green LEDs: Vf ≈ 1.8–2.2V  |  Blue/White LEDs: Vf ≈ 3.0–3.5V

Standard current is 20mA for full brightness. Use 5–10mA for low-power or indicator use. Always round up to the next standard resistor value (E12 or E24 series).

// RC Time Constant

One time constant (τ) is the time for a capacitor to charge to 63.2% of supply voltage. After 5τ the capacitor is considered fully charged (99.3%). Energy is stored in joules: E = ½CV² where C is in Farads.

// Low-Pass vs High-Pass

Same formula, different configuration. In a low-pass filter, the capacitor is in parallel with the load — signals above fc are attenuated. In a high-pass filter, the capacitor is in series — signals below fc are attenuated. The cutoff frequency is the same in both cases.

// Efficiency Note

Real-world battery life is 70–85% of theoretical due to voltage cutoff, temperature, and self-discharge. The efficiency factor accounts for this. Use 80% for a realistic estimate, 100% for ideal/theoretical max.

// Resistor Power Rating

When selecting a resistor, always choose one with a power rating at least 2× the calculated dissipation. Standard ratings: 1/8W, 1/4W, 1/2W, 1W, 2W, 5W. Underrated resistors overheat and fail — or cause fires.
